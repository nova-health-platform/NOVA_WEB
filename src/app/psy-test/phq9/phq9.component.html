PHQ-9 – Depression Screening

The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) is a nine-item depression screening and diagnostic tool developed to assess the presence and severity of depressive symptoms in adults. Originally introduced in 2001, it is commonly used in primary care settings.

The PHQ-9 can function both as a stand-alone tool and as part of the larger Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ). It is part of a broader suite of tools designed for mental health evaluation in primary care.

The test takes less than three minutes to complete and is scored by summing the responses to the nine items. Each item corresponds to a symptom of depression as outlined in the DSM-5. The PHQ-9 is widely used by healthcare professionals to screen for possible depression.

⚠️ These tools are not medical diagnoses. They are self-assessments meant to guide and inform. If you’re struggling, consider seeking help from a mental health professional.

Understanding Your Results

  • 0 – 4: None-minimal — No action needed.
  • 5 – 9: Mild — Repeat PHQ-9 at follow-up.
  • 10 – 14: Moderate — Consider counseling, follow-up, and/or treatment.
  • 15 – 19: Moderately Severe — Recommend medication and counseling.
  • 20 – 27: Severe — Medication and urgent referral to mental health specialist.

  • 0 – 4: None-minimal — No action needed.
    This score range indicates very few or no depressive symptoms. No treatment or follow-up is usually necessary unless symptoms worsen.
  • 5 – 9: Mild — Repeat PHQ-9 at follow-up.
    Some mild depressive symptoms are present. Monitoring and a follow-up screening are recommended to assess persistence or progression.
  • 10 – 14: Moderate — Consider counseling, follow-up, and/or treatment.
    Symptoms suggest moderate depression. It's appropriate to develop a treatment plan that may include psychotherapy, support, or medication.
  • 15 – 19: Moderately Severe — Recommend medication and counseling.
    Symptoms are likely to interfere significantly with daily life. A combination of antidepressant medication and counseling is generally advised.
  • 20 – 27: Severe — Medication and urgent referral to mental health specialist.
    This indicates severe depression. Immediate intervention is needed with medication and a referral to a mental health professional for comprehensive care.

This is a self-assessment tool, not a diagnosis. Please consult a mental health professional if needed.
